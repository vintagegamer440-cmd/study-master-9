Chapter 1: How I Taught My Grandmother to Read

Original companion notes · Story by Sudha Murty · Companion poem: “Bharat Our Land” by Subramania Bharati

These are original revision notes and practice questions, not a reproduction of the textbook. Check your school’s prescribed edition and use the official textbook for the complete lesson and original exercises.

1. Important questions with model answers

Q1. Why did the grandmother want to learn to read?
She wanted to read the magazine story on her own instead of depending on her granddaughter. She understood that reading would make her more independent.
Q2. What made the grandmother realise her dependence?
When her granddaughter was away, she could not read the next instalment of the story herself. This made her feel how important literacy was to her.
Q3. How did the granddaughter help her grandmother?
She patiently taught her the letters and helped her practise reading until she could read independently.
Q4. What does the grandmother’s goal teach us?
It teaches us to set a clear target, practise regularly and remain determined even when we begin something new later in life.
Q5. Why is the ending meaningful?
The grandmother shows respect to her granddaughter as her teacher. It highlights that teaching is worthy of respect regardless of the teacher’s age.
Q6. Suggest a suitable message for the story.
Education creates confidence and independence, and it is never too late to learn.

2. Key facts, setting and timeline

  • Author: Sudha Murty.
  • Setting: A village in north Karnataka; the family reads a Kannada magazine serial.
  • Story within the chapter: Kashi Yatre, a serialised story the grandmother enjoys.
  • Important occasion: Dassara is the grandmother’s learning-goal deadline.
  • Date note: The lesson does not specify an exact calendar year or date. The date of Dassara changes each year.
  • Inventions and discoveries: No particular invention or scientific discovery is central to this story. Focus on literacy, determination and independence.

3. Learning goals

  • Explain why the grandmother decides to become literate in later life.
  • Describe the relationship between the narrator and her grandmother.
  • Discuss literacy, independence, determination and respect for teachers.
  • Understand the central idea and patriotic tone of the companion poem.
  • Practise vocabulary, comprehension and paragraph writing.

4. Story explained in simple English

The narrator remembers her childhood in a village in north Karnataka, where she lived with her grandparents. The family eagerly waited for newspapers and a weekly magazine to arrive by bus. The magazine carried a serialised story called Kashi Yatre, which the grandmother loved.

The grandmother could not read because she had not received formal schooling when she was young. She depended on her granddaughter to read each instalment aloud. When the granddaughter had to be away for a few days, the grandmother could not continue reading the story herself. This experience made her realise how much she wanted to read independently.

She decided to learn the Kannada alphabet and set herself a clear goal: she would be able to read by the festival of Dassara. Her granddaughter became her teacher. The grandmother practised with discipline and determination. When she finally read independently, she showed deep respect for her young teacher. The story reminds readers that learning has no age limit and education helps people become independent.

5. Quick short notes

  • Central idea: Education builds confidence and independence.
  • Grandmother: Determined, eager to learn, humble and disciplined.
  • Granddaughter: Patient and supportive; she becomes the grandmother’s teacher.
  • Turning point: The grandmother cannot read the next magazine instalment when her granddaughter is away.
  • Goal: Learn to read before Dassara.
  • Message: It is never too late to learn; regular practice helps us reach a goal.

6. Main characters

The grandmother

She is affectionate, determined, eager to learn and humble. She recognises a difficulty in her life and takes responsibility for overcoming it.

The granddaughter / narrator

She is caring, patient and supportive. She takes her grandmother’s wish seriously and teaches her with encouragement.

7. Themes and values

  • Lifelong learning: A person can begin learning at any age.
  • Literacy and independence: Reading gives people greater control over everyday life.
  • Determination: A clear goal and regular practice help overcome difficulties.
  • Respect for teachers: A teacher deserves respect because of the role of teaching, not because of age.
  • Family support: Patient encouragement can help someone achieve a meaningful goal.

8. Important vocabulary

  • Illiterate: unable to read and write.
  • Determined: firmly decided to do something.
  • Independent: able to do things without depending on others.
  • Eager: very interested and excited to do something.
  • Embarrassed: feeling shy or uncomfortable.
  • Instalment: one part of a story published at a time.
  • Appreciate: recognise the value of something.
  • Respect: show regard for someone or something.

9. Companion poem: “Bharat Our Land”

Central idea: The poem expresses pride in India’s natural beauty, cultural richness and achievements. It presents the country with admiration and encourages readers to value their homeland.

Tone: Patriotic, appreciative and celebratory.

Revision points: Identify the images of nature and achievement used by the poet. Explain how these images create a feeling of pride. Support answers with brief references to the poem rather than memorising long passages.

10. Practice questions

1. Choose the best answer:

What was the grandmother’s main motivation?

A. To win a competition
B. To read independently
C. To become a writer
D. To travel alone

Answer: B. To read independently.
2. True or false:

The story suggests that learning is possible only during childhood.

Answer: False. Learning can take place at any age.
3. Short answer:

Write two qualities shown by the grandmother.

Answer: Determination and eagerness to learn.
4. Vocabulary:

Use the word “independent” in an original sentence.

Sample: Learning to read helped the grandmother become more independent.
5. Writing:

Write 80–100 words on “Why should we never stop learning?”

Include lifelong learning, confidence, useful skills, independence and one everyday example.
6. Fill in the blanks:
  1. The grandmother wanted to read the story ________.
  2. Her target was to learn before the festival of ________.
  3. The granddaughter became her ________.
Answers: independently; Dassara; teacher.
7. Match the following:

A. Grandmother — 1. Patient teacher
B. Granddaughter — 2. Eager learner
C. Literacy — 3. Independence

Answers: A–2, B–1, C–3.
8. Very short answers:

a) What skill did the grandmother wish to gain? b) Who taught her? c) Name the festival she chose as her target.

Answers: Reading; her granddaughter; Dassara.
9. Assertion and reason:

Assertion: The grandmother wanted to learn to read independently.
Reason: She did not want to depend on someone else to enjoy the magazine story.

Both statements are true, and the reason explains the assertion.

11. Long-answer practice

Q1. Explain how the grandmother’s determination helped her become literate.
Model answer points: She recognised her dependence, chose a clear target, practised the Kannada alphabet regularly, accepted help from her granddaughter and continued until she could read by herself.
Q2. “There is no age limit for learning.” Explain with reference to the story.
Model answer points: The grandmother begins learning as an adult. She does not allow her age or lack of earlier schooling to stop her. Her progress shows that interest, practice and support can make learning possible at different stages of life.
Q3. Describe the bond between the grandmother and the narrator.
Model answer points: Their relationship is loving and trusting. The narrator reads to her grandmother and later teaches her. The grandmother values her help and respects her as a teacher.

Chapter 9 — Propositions and their Converses

A proposition is a statement with a definite truth value. The converse of “If P, then Q” is “If Q, then P”. A counterexample disproves a universal claim.

Important: Give the converse of “If a number is divisible by 4, it is even”. Is it true?
Converse: If a number is even, it is divisible by 4. False; 6 is even but not divisible by 4.
Fill blanks: A statement with a truth value is a proposition; converse of If P then Q is If Q then P; one counterexample disproves a universal claim.
True/False: Converse of a true statement is always true — False. “7 is prime” is a proposition — True. 2 disproves “all primes are odd” — True.
MCQ: Counterexample to “every odd number is prime”: (A)3 (B)5 (C)9 (D)7. Answer: C.
Case: If divisible by 5 then ends in 0? False; 15 is divisible by 5 but ends in 5.
Long answer: Test “If a number is a multiple of 10, it is even” and its converse.
The original is true because multiples of 10 are divisible by 2. Converse: every even number is a multiple of 10; false, since 8 is even but not a multiple of 10.

Chapter 10 — How Quantities Combine: Understanding Data

Weighted mean = sum(value × frequency) ÷ sum(frequencies). Group sizes matter when combining means. Stacked bars show how categories contribute to totals.

Important: 20 students have mean 60 and 30 students have mean 70. Find combined mean.
(20×60+30×70)/50 = 66.
Fill blanks: Mean = sum of observations ÷ number of observations; weighted mean accounts for group size; stacked bars show components.
True/False: Directly averaging unequal-sized group means is always correct — False. Sum of frequencies is total observations — True.
MCQ: Mean of 4,6,8 is (A)5 (B)6 (C)7 (D)8. Answer B.
Case: Class A: 15 learners, mean 72; Class B: 25 learners, mean 80. Combined mean = (15×72+25×80)/40 = 77.
Long answer: Why use weighted mean?
A larger group contributes more observations, so its mean must carry proportionally more weight. Multiply each mean by its group size, add, then divide by total size.

Chapter 11 — The World of Algorithms

An algorithm is a finite, clear sequence of steps. Euclid’s algorithm finds GCD using repeated division and remainders.

Important: Find GCD(48,18).
48=2×18+12; 18=1×12+6; 12=2×6+0. GCD=6.
Fill blanks: An algorithm has finite steps; GCD divides both numbers exactly; stop when remainder is zero.
True/False: Algorithm steps may be ambiguous — False. Euclid uses division with remainder — True.
MCQ: GCD(24,36): (A)6 (B)12 (C)18 (D)24. Answer B.
Case: 36 pencils and 48 erasers are packed into identical kits without leftovers. Maximum kits = GCD(36,48)=12; each has 3 pencils and 4 erasers.
Long answer: Find GCD(84,30).
84=2×30+24; 30=1×24+6; 24=4×6+0. GCD=6.

Chapter 12 — Quadrilaterals

Interior angles of a quadrilateral total 360°. A parallelogram has opposite sides parallel and equal; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other. Triangle midpoint theorem: the segment joining midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half its length.

Important: Three angles are 85°,95°,110°. Find the fourth.
360°−290°=70°.
Fill blanks: Angle sum 360°; opposite sides of parallelogram are parallel; diagonals bisect each other.
True/False: Every rectangle is a parallelogram — True. Diagonals of every parallelogram are equal — False.
MCQ: Adjacent angle to 65° in a parallelogram: (A)65 (B)115 (C)125 (D)295. Answer B.
Case: Parallelogram garden sides 9m and 6m, one angle 72°. Adjacent angle 108°, opposite angle 72°, perimeter 30m.
Long answer: State midpoint theorem.
In triangle ABC, if D and E are midpoints of AB and AC, then DE ∥ BC and DE=½BC.

Chapter 13 — Two Variables, One Line

A linear equation ax+by=c has ordered-pair solutions that lie on a straight line. Slope is change in y divided by change in x.

Important: Give three solutions of 2x+y=6.
(0,6), (1,4), (3,0).
Fill blanks: Graph is a straight line; (2,1) satisfies x+y=3; slope is change in y/change in x.
True/False: A linear equation in two variables can have many solutions — True. A horizontal line has slope 0 — True.
MCQ: Slope of horizontal line: (A)0 (B)1 (C)undefined (D)−1. Answer A.
Case: Two notebooks at ₹x each and one pen at ₹y cost ₹50. Equation 2x+y=50; if x=20 then y=10; (15,20) satisfies it.
Long answer: Table for x+y=4.
For x=0,1,2,3,4, y=4,3,2,1,0. Plot (0,4),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1),(4,0) and join them to make a straight line.

Chapter 14 — Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume

Formulae: Cuboid V=lbh, TSA=2(lb+bh+hl); cube V=a³, TSA=6a²; cylinder V=πr²h, CSA=2πrh; cone V=⅓πr²h, slant height √(r²+h²), CSA=πrl; sphere V=4/3πr³, area=4πr²; hemisphere V=2/3πr³, TSA=3πr².

Important: Volume of cuboid 5×4×3 cm?
60 cm³.
Fill blanks: Cube volume a³; cylinder CSA 2πrh; cone volume ⅓πr²h; surface area uses square units.
True/False: Volume uses cubic units — True. Equal volumes guarantee equal surface areas — False.
MCQ: Cube side 3cm volume: (A)9 (B)18 (C)27 (D)36. Answer C.
Case: Cylinder radius 2m, height 3m, π=3.14. Volume=37.68m³; curved area=37.68m².
Long answer: Cone radius 3cm, height 4cm. Find slant height, CSA and volume.
l=√(9+16)=5cm. CSA=πrl=3.14×3×5=47.1cm². Volume=⅓×3.14×9×4=37.68cm³.

Mixed Revision Test

  1. Give a counterexample to “all even numbers are multiples of 4”.
  2. Find mean of 5,7,9,11.
  3. Find GCD(56,42).
  4. Find fourth angle if others are 80°,90°,100°.
  5. Give two solutions of 2x+y=7.
  6. Volume of cube side 4cm?
Answers: 1. 6; 2. 8; 3. 14; 4. 90°; 5. (0,7),(1,5); 6. 64cm³.
